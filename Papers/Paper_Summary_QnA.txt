[C1] CONFERENCE PAPER 1

TITLE   : Word-level Deep Sign Language Recognition from Video:
          A New Large-scale Dataset and Methods Comparison
AUTHORS : Dongxu Li, Cristian Rodriguez, Xin Yu, Hongdong Li
VENUE   : WACV (Winter Conference on Applications of Computer Vision) 2020
FILE    : 1910.11006v2.pdf
YEAR    : 2020 (arXiv: October 2019)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This paper introduced WLASL (Word-Level American Sign Language), which at the
time of release was the LARGEST publicly available video dataset for word-level
American Sign Language recognition. Before WLASL, datasets were tiny (fewer
than 100 word classes), making it impossible to train any real-world system.

Key Contributions:
  - WLASL Dataset: 2,000+ common ASL words/glosses, performed by 100+ signers,
    recorded across diverse real-world backgrounds.
  - Two Baseline Approaches Compared:
      1. Holistic Appearance-Based (raw RGB video frames via 3D CNNs like I3D)
      2. Pose-Based (2D skeleton/joint coordinates from body pose estimators)
  - Pose-TGCN: A novel Pose-based Temporal Graph Convolution Network proposed
    in this paper that models spatial-temporal dependencies across joint graphs.
  - Benchmark: Evaluated on WLASL-100, WLASL-300, WLASL-1000, WLASL-2000
    subsets, establishing the first large-scale SLR benchmark for ASL.

Dataset Statistics:
  - 21,083 video clips total
  - 2,000 ASL gloss classes
  - 119 different signers
  - Real-world recording conditions (varied lighting, backgrounds, speeds)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW IT SUPPORTS OUR PROJECT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. DIRECT DATASET USE: Our offline I3D pipeline is trained on WLASL-2000
     (all 2000 classes). This paper IS the source paper for our core dataset.

  2. ARCHITECTURE VALIDATION: The paper benchmarks I3D on WLASL, which is
     exactly the model we use in Pipeline A (the Cloud-Assisted Offline System).
     This validates our choice of I3D for video-based sign recognition.

  3. POSE vs APPEARANCE COMPARISON: The paper's comparison of pose-based vs
     appearance-based approaches directly motivates our DUAL pipeline design:
     - Pipeline A (I3D) = holistic appearance approach
     - Pipeline B (MediaPipe keypoints) = pose-based approach
     Our project essentially implements BOTH approaches the paper compared!

  4. SLIDING WINDOW JUSTIFICATION: The paper discusses temporal windowing for
     video processing, which we use (stride=8, frames=64 windows in Pipeline A).

  5. SCALE JUSTIFICATION: Proves why 2000 classes are needed for real-world
     coverage. Directly answers "why so many classes?"

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q&A - QUESTIONS YOUR PROFESSOR MAY ASK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q1: Why did you choose WLASL as your dataset?
A : WLASL is the largest publicly available benchmark for word-level ASL
    recognition (Li et al., 2020). It contains 21,083 clips across 2,000
    glosses by 119 signers in real-world conditions, making it the gold
    standard for training large-vocabulary sign recognition models.

Q2: What is I3D and why did you use it?
A : I3D (Inflated 3D Convolutions) was benchmarked by the WLASL authors as
    one of the best architectures for holistic video-based sign recognition.
    It extends 2D image classification networks into 3D to capture both
    spatial appearance AND temporal motion simultaneously. The WLASL paper
    established I3D as the strong baseline for this task.

Q3: What is the difference between holistic and pose-based approaches?
A : Holistic approaches (like I3D) process raw RGB video pixels - they are
    powerful but computationally heavy. Pose-based approaches extract skeleton
    keypoints (like MediaPipe landmarks) - they are lighter and more
    privacy-preserving but lose texture information. The WLASL paper compared
    both; we implement BOTH in a dual-pipeline architecture.

Q4: Why does WLASL matter compared to older datasets?
A : Older datasets had <100 word classes. WLASL with 2000 classes is the
    first step toward real-world vocabulary coverage, enabling practical
    ASL recognition systems beyond toy demonstrations.

Q5: What is Pose-TGCN proposed in this paper?
A : It is a Temporal Graph Convolution Network that treats body joints as
    graph nodes and models how they interact both spatially (which joints
    relate to each other) and temporally (how joints move over time frames).
    This inspired our hybrid model's temporal sequence processing approach.

Q6: What were the benchmark accuracy results on WLASL?
A : I3D achieved ~65% Top-1 accuracy on WLASL-100. Performance drops as
    vocabulary grows (WLASL-2000 is significantly harder). This shows the
    challenge and motivates our noise-reduction stabilization filter.

TITLE   : ASL Citizen: A Community-Sourced Dataset for Advancing
          Isolated Sign Language Recognition
AUTHORS : Aashaka Desai, Lauren Berger, Fyodor Minakov, et al.
          (Microsoft Research, University of Washington, University of
          Maryland, Boston University)
VENUE   : NeurIPS 2023 - Datasets and Benchmarks Track
FILE    : NeurIPS-2023-asl-citizen-...-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf
YEAR    : 2023

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ASL Citizen is a large-scale, crowdsourced, CONSENT-DRIVEN American Sign
Language dataset, designed with ethical considerations and community involvement
at its core. It is one of the largest crowdsourced ISLR (Isolated Sign Language
Recognition) datasets ever created.

Key Contributions:
  - ASL Citizen Dataset:
      * 83,399 video clips
      * 2,731 distinct ASL signs
      * 52 everyday signers (not professional/lab signers)
      * Recorded across 16 U.S. states via personal webcams
  - New Task Framing: Proposes "Sign Language Dictionary Retrieval" -
    a user queries a sign via webcam, and the system returns matching
    dictionary entries. More realistic than pure classification.
  - Ethical Dataset: Gathered with explicit IRB approval and informed consent.
    Deaf researchers were active team members throughout. No scraped data.
  - Diversity: Real-world lighting, backgrounds, camera angles, speeds -
    unlike controlled lab-recorded datasets.
  - Signer-Independent Evaluation: Test signers are ENTIRELY unseen during
    training, mimicking real-world deployment where new users appear.
  - Baseline Results: ~63% Top-1 accuracy, ~91% Recall@10 with appearance
    and pose-based classifiers.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW IT SUPPORTS OUR PROJECT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. DIRECT DATASET USE: Our real-time Pipeline B (MediaPipe + Hybrid Model)
     is trained on the ASL Citizen dataset. The 300 best classes in our
     'best_hybrid_300.keras' model come from this dataset.

  2. THE "300 CLASSES" DECISION: The ASL Citizen paper itself shows that
     recognition accuracy drops as vocabulary grows. Our decision to filter
     to the top 300 most distinct classes (F1 >= 0.70) is supported by
     this paper's analysis of class difficulty and diversity.

  3. SIGNER-INDEPENDENT CHALLENGE: The paper introduces the signer-independent
     evaluation protocol. Our live model faces the exact same challenge:
     it must work for ANY user who sits in front of the webcam. This paper
     validates why this is a hard, important problem.

  4. CROWDSOURCED DIVERSITY: ASL Citizen's diverse recording conditions
     (home webcams, 16 states) directly mirrors our use case. Users will
     sign in front of home/office webcams - not in controlled labs. This
     justifies our dataset choice over lab-controlled alternatives.

  5. ETHICAL AI: The consent-driven nature of ASL Citizen aligns with
     responsible AI principles. Professors may ask about ethics - this paper
     gives you a strong answer.

  6. COMMUNITY SOURCED = REAL WORLD: Lab-recorded datasets have professional
     signers with perfect form. ASL Citizen's everyday signers make the model
     robust to real user variability - exactly what our live system needs.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q&A - QUESTIONS YOUR PROFESSOR MAY ASK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q1: Why did you choose ASL Citizen for the real-time pipeline?
A : ASL Citizen (Desai et al., NeurIPS 2023) is one of the largest
    crowdsourced ISLR datasets with 83,399 clips from 52 everyday signers
    across real-world webcam conditions. Unlike lab datasets, it reflects
    how actual users will sign in front of their own cameras, making it
    ideal for our live inference system.

Q2: Why only 300 classes in the real-time model, not all 2,731?
A : The ASL Citizen paper itself demonstrates that recognition accuracy
    degrades with vocabulary size under signer-independent conditions. We
    applied an F1-Score filter (F1 >= 0.70 per class) to select only the 300
    most distinctly recognizable classes. This reduces false positives and
    "jitter" in live inference, ensuring the system only predicts what it's
    confident about.

Q3: What makes ASL Citizen different from WLASL?
A : WLASL was professionally scraped from the internet (YouTube, etc.) with
    controlled recording conditions. ASL Citizen is community-sourced with
    explicit consent, recorded on personal webcams by everyday signers.
    ASL Citizen better represents real users, while WLASL's scale (2000 classes)
    is better for offline high-vocabulary systems.

Q4: How does your system handle new users (signer independence)?
A : This is a core challenge highlighted by ASL Citizen paper's
    signer-independent evaluation protocol. Our Pipeline B addresses this
    through: (1) training on 52 diverse signers, (2) MediaPipe keypoint
    normalization which makes the input signer-agnostic, and (3) restricting
    to 300 high-F1 classes for robust inference.

Q5: What are the ethical considerations of your dataset?
A : We used ASL Citizen which is fully IRB-approved, consent-driven, and
    developed WITH Deaf community researchers. This ensures our system is
    built responsibly and the training data respects signers' rights.

Q6: What is the "dictionary retrieval" task proposed in this paper?
A : Instead of pure classification, it frames recognition as retrieval:
    given a signed query, return the top-K matching dictionary entries.
    This is more practical since users may not sign perfectly - returning
    ranked candidates is more forgiving. Our system can be extended to
    support this in future work.

[T1] TRANSACTION PAPER 1

TITLE   : StepNet: Spatial-temporal Part-aware Network for
          Isolated Sign Language Recognition
AUTHORS : Xiaolong Shen, Zhedong Zheng, Yi Yang
VENUE   : ACM Transactions on Multimedia Computing, Communications,
          and Applications (ACM TOMM) - 2023
FILE    : 2212.12857v2.pdf
YEAR    : 2023 (arXiv: December 2022, Published in ACM TOMM 2023)

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
StepNet addresses a key limitation in sign language recognition: standard
video recognition models treat all body regions equally, but signs are
defined by fine-grained movements in SPECIFIC body parts (mainly hands and
face). StepNet explicitly models which parts matter and when.

Key Contributions:
  - Part-Aware Feature Extraction: Identifies and separately encodes
    discriminative body parts (hands, face, arms) rather than processing
    the whole frame uniformly.
  - Spatial-Temporal Modeling: Models BOTH where (spatial part location)
    AND when (temporal progression) discriminative cues occur across frames.
  - Part Attention Mechanism: A learnable attention module that assigns
    higher weights to informative regions at each timestep.
  - Evaluated on: WLASL-2000, NMFs-CSL, and SLR500 benchmarks.
  - Strong Results: Outperforms prior state-of-the-art on WLASL with
    significantly better handling of fine-grained hand/finger movements.

Core Architecture:
  Input Video -> Part Detection -> Spatial Feature Extraction per Part
  -> Temporal Sequence Modeling -> Part Attention Fusion -> Classification

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW IT SUPPORTS OUR PROJECT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. THEORETICAL GROUNDING FOR HYBRID MODEL: Our 'best_hybrid_300.keras'
     is a hybrid architecture that processes MediaPipe landmarks (which are
     inherently PART-BASED - 21 hand landmarks per hand + pose landmarks).
     StepNet provides the academic justification that part-aware approaches
     outperform whole-frame approaches for SLR.

  2. WHY MEDIAPIPE (PART-BASED) WORKS: StepNet proves that focusing on
     specific body parts (hands especially) is the right approach. MediaPipe
     extracts exactly this - 126 hand landmarks + 99 pose landmarks. Our
     keypoint selection directly reflects StepNet's insights.

  3. SPATIAL + TEMPORAL MODELING: Our hybrid model processes a rolling
     window of 50 frames of keypoint sequences - this is spatial (3D
     coordinates of landmarks) + temporal (sequence over frames) modeling,
     exactly what StepNet advocates.

  4. SAME BENCHMARK: StepNet is evaluated on WLASL-2000, the same dataset
     our offline pipeline uses. This creates a direct comparison point.

  5. PART ATTENTION = OUR HEURISTIC GESTURES: StepNet's part attention
     (knowing which parts matter when) is mirrored in our heuristic gesture
     controls - e.g., we specifically monitor thumb tip coordinates for
     "Thumbs Up" gesture and palm coordinates for "Open Palm", showing
     spatial part awareness in our design.

  6. TRANSACTION PAPER CREDIBILITY: ACM TOMM is a Q1 journal in multimedia
     computing. Publishing in a transaction journal requires extensive
     experiments, ablation studies, and reproducibility - it signals
     thorough, rigorous research to your evaluation committee.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q&A - QUESTIONS YOUR PROFESSOR MAY ASK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q1: What is the core idea behind StepNet and why is it relevant?
A : StepNet (Shen et al., ACM TOMM 2023) argues that sign recognition
    depends on fine-grained movements of specific body parts, not the
    whole frame. It proposes a spatial-temporal part-aware network that
    explicitly focuses on hands and face. This directly motivates our
    choice of MediaPipe for part-level keypoint extraction rather than
    processing raw video pixels in the real-time pipeline.

Q2: How does your hybrid model relate to StepNet's architecture?
A : StepNet uses: (1) part detection, (2) per-part spatial feature
    extraction, and (3) temporal sequence modeling. Our hybrid model
    equivalently uses: (1) MediaPipe for part landmark detection,
    (2) 3D coordinate extraction per landmark (spatial), and (3) a
    sequential neural network on 50-frame rolling windows (temporal).
    Our architecture is a lightweight edge-deployable version of StepNet's
    core principles.

Q3: Why is part-aware modeling important for sign language?
A : Signs differ in subtle ways - two signs may look identical except for
    a finger position or hand orientation. Whole-frame processing "dilutes"
    these fine-grained differences with irrelevant background information.
    Part-aware processing, as proven by StepNet, allows the model to
    focus computation on what actually distinguishes one sign from another.

Q4: What datasets did StepNet use and how do you compare?
A : StepNet was evaluated on WLASL-2000 (among others). Our offline pipeline
    also uses WLASL-2000. StepNet represents the academic state-of-the-art
    for this benchmark, and our system builds on its findings.

Q5: What is ACM TOMM and why does it matter?
A : ACM Transactions on Multimedia Computing, Communications, and
    Applications is a peer-reviewed Q1 journal published by the Association
    for Computing Machinery (ACM). Transaction papers require much more
    extensive validation than conference papers, meaning StepNet's claims
    are thoroughly tested and reproducible.

Q6: How does StepNet's temporal modeling compare to your approach?
A : StepNet uses temporal convolutions and attention over video frames.
    Our Pipeline B uses a rolling buffer of 50 keypoint frames fed into
    a hybrid neural network. Both model the TEMPORAL PROGRESSION of signs
    (how joints move over time), which is essential since signs are dynamic
    gestures, not static poses.

TITLE   : SignBERT+: Hand-Model-Aware Self-Supervised Pre-Training
          for Sign Language Understanding
AUTHORS : Hezhen Hu, Weichao Zhao, Wengang Zhou, Yunyao Wang,
          Houqiang Li
VENUE   : IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence
          (IEEE TPAMI) - 2023
FILE    : SignBERT_Hand-Model-Aware_Self-Supervised_Pre-Training_for_
          Sign_Language_Understanding.pdf
YEAR    : 2023

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SignBERT+ is a self-supervised pre-training framework specifically designed
for sign language understanding using hand pose representations (keypoints/
landmarks). It adapts the BERT-style masked self-supervised learning paradigm
to the domain of sign language hand models.

Key Contributions:
  - Hand-Model-Aware Pre-Training: Instead of pre-training on raw pixels,
    SignBERT+ pre-trains on 3D HAND POSE MODELS (skeleton/keypoint sequences),
    making it directly applicable to landmark-based recognition systems.
  - BERT-Style Masking: During pre-training, random hand joints are MASKED
    and the model learns to reconstruct them from context - teaching it
    the natural spatial relationships between finger joints.
  - Self-Supervised: Does NOT require labeled sign data for pre-training.
    Can leverage any hand video (even non-sign language videos) to learn
    general hand motion representations.
  - Multi-Task Understanding: Handles Isolated SLR, Continuous SLR, and
    Sign Language Production (generation) within a unified framework.
  - Hand Context Module (HCM): Models fine-grained interactions between
    finger joints, palm, and wrist - the most critical articulators in
    sign language.
  - Outperforms state-of-the-art on: MSASL, WLASL, SLR500, and Phoenix-2014.

Pre-Training Process:
  Unlabeled Hand Videos -> Extract 3D Hand Landmarks -> Randomly Mask Joints
  -> Transformer Encoder -> Reconstruct Masked Joints -> Learn Hand Priors

Fine-Tuning Process:
  Pre-trained Weights -> Sign Language Videos (Labeled) -> Task-Specific Head
  -> Classification / Translation Output

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
HOW IT SUPPORTS OUR PROJECT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. DIRECT ALIGNMENT WITH PIPELINE B: Our real-time pipeline extracts
     exactly what SignBERT+ operates on - 3D hand landmark coordinates
     from MediaPipe (21 landmarks per hand = 63 values, times 2 hands =
     126 values). SignBERT+ proves this is the RIGHT representation space.

  2. JUSTIFIES KEYPOINT-BASED APPROACH OVER PIXEL-BASED: SignBERT+ shows
     that hand pose representations alone (WITHOUT RGB pixels) are powerful
     enough for high-accuracy sign recognition. This validates our Pipeline B
     design decision to use keypoints rather than raw video in real-time.

  3. LIMITED DATA JUSTIFICATION: Our ASL Citizen 300-class model trains on
     a relatively small labeled set. SignBERT+'s self-supervised approach
     shows that hand pose models can be pre-trained without labels, then
     fine-tuned - justifying why our approach works even with limited
     labeled data per class.

  4. UNDERSTANDING HAND JOINT RELATIONSHIPS: SignBERT+'s Hand Context Module
     (HCM) models interactions between finger joints. Our MediaPipe extraction
     captures 21 landmarks per hand - the same joint structure. This validates
     that joint-level coordinate features are semantically rich.

  5. FEATURE DIMENSIONALITY MATCH: SignBERT+ uses 63-dim hand vectors (21
     joints x 3D). Our pipeline uses 126 (two hands x 63) + 99 pose = 225
     total features per frame. We extend SignBERT+'s hand-only framework
     with full body context.

  6. HIGHEST JOURNAL PRESTIGE: IEEE TPAMI (Impact Factor ~23.6) is THE most
     prestigious journal in computer vision. Having a TPAMI paper as a
     reference immediately elevates the academic credibility of your project
     in the eyes of any professor or reviewer.

  7. BERT PARADIGM RELEVANCE: Our gloss-to-sentence step uses LLMs
     (Gemini API / FLAN-T5). BERT is the foundation of all modern LLMs.
     SignBERT+ bridges BERT-style pre-training with sign language, showing
     that the NLP pre-training paradigm is applicable end-to-end in your
     system (from keypoints to sentences).

Q&A - QUESTIONS YOUR PROFESSOR MAY ASK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q1: What is SignBERT+ and why is it relevant to your project?
A : SignBERT+ (Hu et al., IEEE TPAMI 2023) is a self-supervised pre-training
    framework for sign language recognition using 3D hand pose representations
    (keypoints). It proves that hand landmark sequences are a powerful and
    sufficient representation for sign understanding - directly validating
    our MediaPipe-based real-time pipeline which uses the same 3D hand
    landmark coordinates (21 per hand).

Q2: What is self-supervised pre-training and why does it matter?
A : Self-supervised learning (SSL) allows a model to learn from UNLABELED
    data by defining pretext tasks (like reconstructing masked joints).
    SignBERT+ uses this to learn general hand motion priors without needing
    thousands of labeled sign videos. This is crucial for our project because
    sign language datasets are inherently limited in size - SSL allows
    learning rich representations from even limited labeled data.

Q3: How does SignBERT+ use BERT-style masking for hand poses?
A : Just as BERT masks words in a sentence and predicts them from context,
    SignBERT+ masks random hand joints (e.g., middle finger tip) and trains
    the model to reconstruct them from surrounding joints and temporal context.
    This forces the model to learn the natural biomechanical constraints of
    hands - e.g., if the middle finger is bent, the ring finger is likely
    nearby. These priors help downstream sign recognition enormously.

Q4: What is the IEEE TPAMI and why is it important?
A : IEEE Transactions on Pattern Analysis and Machine Intelligence is the
    flagship journal of the IEEE Computer Society and the most prestigious
    publication venue in computer vision and pattern recognition (Impact
    Factor ~23.6). Papers published here have undergone extremely rigorous
    peer review. Citing TPAMI work immediately establishes the scientific
    credibility of your approach.

Q5: How does your project extend SignBERT+'s approach?
A : SignBERT+ focuses on hand-only representations. Our Pipeline B extends
    this with: (1) full body pose (99 additional MediaPipe landmarks),
    (2) a rolling temporal buffer of 50 frames for real-time inference,
    (3) heuristic spatial gesture controls (using coordinate thresholds),
    and (4) integration with an offline LLM (FLAN-T5) for gloss-to-sentence
    conversion. We take SignBERT+'s validated representation and build a
    complete end-to-end translation system around it.

Q6: Why 3D coordinates and not just 2D hand images?
A : SignBERT+ demonstrates that 3D pose representations (x, y, z per joint)
    provide crucial depth information - e.g., whether a palm faces the camera
    or faces away changes meaning in many signs. Raw 2D images lose this
    depth cue. MediaPipe's 3D landmarks (which we use) preserve this depth
    information, aligning with SignBERT+'s finding that 3D representation
    is superior for sign language understanding.

Q7: What tasks does SignBERT+ handle beyond recognition?
A : SignBERT+ handles Isolated SLR, Continuous SLR, AND Sign Language
    Production. Our project similarly addresses recognition (both pipelines)
    plus natural language generation (via Gemini API / FLAN-T5). This
    parallel scope supports our broader project vision.

QUESTION: "Why did you build this project? What gap does it fill?"
-----------------------------------------------------------------
WLASL (C1): Despite 70M+ deaf people worldwide, no large-scale ASL recognition
dataset existed before 2020. Sign language recognition was stuck at toy-level
(<100 words). WLASL enabled the first real-vocabulary ASL recognition research.

ASL Citizen (C2): Even after WLASL, datasets were not diverse enough to work
for real users in real conditions. Lab-recorded, non-consented data could not
generalize. ASL Citizen bridged this gap with community-sourced, ethical data.

StepNet (T1): Recognition architectures treated all video regions equally,
missing the fine-grained hand/finger movements that define signs. Part-aware
spatial-temporal modeling was needed.

SignBERT+ (T2): Pre-training and representation learning was entirely in
the pixel domain (RGB frames). Hand pose as a representation space for
self-supervised learning was unexplored - leaving a gap for lightweight,
privacy-preserving, edge-deployable recognition systems.

OUR CONTRIBUTION: We synthesize all of the above into a complete DUAL-PIPELINE
SYSTEM: (1) a high-fidelity cloud pipeline using I3D on WLASL-2000 + Gemini
API for sentence construction, and (2) a privacy-first edge pipeline using
MediaPipe keypoints on ASL Citizen-300 + offline FLAN-T5. Both gaps identified
by these 4 papers are addressed in our implementation.
