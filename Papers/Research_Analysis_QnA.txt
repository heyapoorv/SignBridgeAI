SECTION 1: THE PROBLEM STATEMENT — WHY THIS RESEARCH EXISTS
================================================================================

Q: What is the core problem your project addresses?
--------------------------------------------------
A: Over 430 million people worldwide have disabling hearing loss (WHO, 2023),
   and approximately 70 million Deaf individuals use sign language as their
   primary mode of communication. However, the vast majority of the hearing
   population does not know any sign language, creating a severe communication
   barrier in everyday situations — hospitals, schools, government offices,
   and public services.

   Existing solutions are inadequate:
   - Human interpreters are expensive, unavailable 24/7, and inaccessible in
     rural or low-resource areas.
   - Early automated systems were limited to <50 word vocabularies, laboratory
     conditions, and required special hardware (data gloves, depth cameras).
   - Modern deep learning systems achieve reasonable accuracy but fail to
     bridge the "gloss-to-sentence" gap — they output raw sign labels (glosses)
     not natural spoken English sentences.
   - No production-ready system works fully offline on standard hardware
     (laptop + webcam) with large vocabulary and natural language output.

   Our project directly addresses all these gaps with a dual-pipeline
   architecture that works both in cloud-assisted and fully offline modes.

Q: What is "Sign Language Recognition" vs "Sign Language Translation"?
----------------------------------------------------------------------
A: Sign Language Recognition (SLR): Identifying WHAT sign is being performed
   — outputs a gloss/label (e.g., "HELLO", "THANK-YOU").
   
   Sign Language Translation (SLT): Converting a sequence of signs into a
   fluent natural language sentence (e.g., "Hello, thank you for coming.").
   
   SLT is significantly harder because:
   1. Sign language grammar is different from English grammar
      (e.g., "ME NAME JOHN" → "My name is John")
   2. Temporal segmentation: knowing where one sign ends and another begins
   3. Co-articulation: signs blend together in continuous signing
   4. Context dependence: same sign may mean different things in context

   Most prior work only addresses SLR. Our project achieves FULL SLT by
   adding LLM-based (Gemini API / FLAN-T5) gloss-to-sentence conversion.

SECTION 2: WHAT HAS RESEARCH ACHIEVED SO FAR?
================================================================================

Q: What has the research community accomplished in this field?
-------------------------------------------------------------
A: Significant progress has been made across four dimensions:

   1. DATASETS:
      - WLASL (Li et al., 2020): First large-scale ASL dataset with 2000+
        glosses, enabling large-vocabulary recognition research.
      - ASL Citizen (Desai et al., NeurIPS 2023): First crowdsourced,
        consent-driven, signer-diverse ASL dataset (83K clips, 2731 signs).
      - PHOENIX-2014T: Standard benchmark for continuous German Sign Language
        translation (widely used for SLT evaluation).
      - BSL-1K: Large British Sign Language dataset with mouthing cues.
      - BOBSL: Massive BBC British Sign Language corpus.
      
   2. RECOGNITION ARCHITECTURES:
      - I3D (Inflated 3D ConvNets): Extends image classification 3D for video,
        captures spatiotemporal features simultaneously.
      - Graph Convolutional Networks (GCNs): Model body skeleton as a graph,
        capture joint-level spatial relationships (Pose-TGCN in WLASL paper).
      - Transformers: Self-attention over frame sequences for global temporal
        context. Now the dominant approach.
      - StepNet (ACM TOMM 2023): Part-aware spatial-temporal modeling —
        explicitly focuses on hands and face.
      - SignBERT+ (IEEE TPAMI 2023): Self-supervised pre-training on hand pose
        models, enabling strong recognition with limited labeled data.

   3. TRANSLATION (GLOSS TO TEXT):
      - Sequence-to-Sequence models (LSTM-based) for gloss-to-text.
      - SLTUNET (ICLR 2023): Joint training of sign-to-gloss + gloss-to-text.
      - SignLLM (CVPR 2024): Using large language models for gloss-free SLT.
      - LLMs can now convert gloss sequences to grammatically correct English
        with impressive accuracy using zero-shot or few-shot prompting.

   4. REAL-TIME SYSTEMS:
      - MediaPipe (Google, 2019-present): Real-time hand/pose landmark
        extraction on CPU, enabling lightweight inference on edge devices.
      - Pose-based classifiers (LSTM, TCN, Transformer) on landmark sequences
        achieve real-time inference at 30+ FPS on standard hardware.

   5. EVALUATION IMPROVEMENTS:
      - Top-K accuracy metrics for large-vocabulary recognition.
      - BLEU, ROUGE scores for translation quality.
      - Signer-independent evaluation protocols (ASL Citizen paper).
      - F1-Score per class for realistic assessment of deployment readiness.

Q: What accuracy levels have been achieved?
------------------------------------------
A: On WLASL Benchmark (Word-Level ASL Recognition):
   - WLASL-100 (100 classes): Best systems reach ~79-85% Top-1 accuracy
   - WLASL-300 (300 classes): ~65-75% Top-1 accuracy
   - WLASL-1000: ~55-65% Top-1 accuracy
   - WLASL-2000 (our pipeline): ~46-55% Top-1 accuracy (harder, more classes)

   On PHOENIX-2014T (Continuous SLT, German Sign Language):
   - BLEU-4 scores: ~25-30 for top systems (as of 2024)
   - Human performance estimated at BLEU-4 ~35-40

   On ASL Citizen (Isolated Recognition, 2731 classes):
   - Baseline: ~63% Top-1 accuracy, ~91% Recall@10
   - Signer-independent evaluation makes this a lower bound in practice

   Real-Time Systems (MediaPipe + LSTM/Hybrid, ~300 classes):
   - High-accuracy classes: F1 > 0.85
   - System-level accuracy when filtered to confident classes: ~75-85%

SECTION 3: RESEARCH GAPS — WHAT HAS NOT BEEN SOLVED?
================================================================================

Q: What are the major research gaps in Sign Language Recognition/Translation?
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
A: Despite impressive progress, significant gaps remain:

   GAP 1: THE GLOSS-TO-SENTENCE PROBLEM (Most Critical)
   -----------------------------------------------------
   Problem: SLR models output glosses — raw sign labels in the order signed.
   Sign language grammar differs fundamentally from spoken English grammar.
   Example: Signer produces [NAME + ME + JOHN] but the English sentence is
   "My name is John." Most SLR systems stop at the gloss stage and cannot
   produce grammatically correct, natural English output.
   
   Status: Partially addressed by LLM-based approaches (SignLLM, SLTUNET),
   but most systems require gloss annotations (expensive to create) and
   still struggle with complex sentences, idioms, and context.
   
   OUR SOLUTION: We use LLMs (Gemini 2.5 Flash / FLAN-T5-Small) to
   directly convert gloss sequences to natural English — no gloss annotation
   needed at inference time.

   GAP 2: VOCABULARY SCALE vs REAL-TIME PERFORMANCE TRADEOFF
   ----------------------------------------------------------
   Problem: High-accuracy systems (I3D, Transformer) require heavy computation
   and cannot run in real-time. Lightweight systems capable of real-time
   inference are limited to small vocabularies (<300 words).
   No single system achieves BOTH large vocabulary AND real-time performance
   on standard consumer hardware (laptop + webcam).
   
   Status: Unresolved in the literature. Edge-optimized models compromise
   heavily on vocabulary or accuracy.
   
   OUR SOLUTION: Dual-pipeline architecture — Pipeline A (I3D, 2000 classes,
   cloud/offline video processing) for high accuracy, Pipeline B (keypoints,
   300 classes, real-time webcam) for low-latency edge deployment. We solve
   the tradeoff by making both available with different use-case targets.

   GAP 3: SIGNER INDEPENDENCE (GENERALIZATION TO UNSEEN USERS)
   -----------------------------------------------------------
   Problem: Most models are trained and tested on the SAME set of signers.
   When a new user (new signing style, different hand size, speed) uses the
   system, accuracy drops significantly. This is the signer-independence gap.
   
   Status: ASL Citizen paper formally defined this as the key evaluation
   challenge. SignBERT+ partially addresses it with pre-training. Full
   signer-independence for large vocabularies remains unsolved.
   
   OUR SOLUTION: MediaPipe landmark normalization makes our input signer-agnostic
   (coordinates are normalized relative to wrist/palm center). F1-filtered
   class selection ensures only robust, generalizable classes are used.

   GAP 4: INTERNET DEPENDENCY & PRIVACY
   ------------------------------------
   Problem: Most modern SLT systems rely on cloud APIs for LLM-based
   translation (GPT, Gemini, etc.), requiring constant internet and sending
   user signing data to external servers — unacceptable for:
   - Privacy-sensitive environments (hospitals, legal proceedings)
   - Rural/remote areas with no internet
   - Offline deployment scenarios
   
   Status: Fully offline, large-vocabulary SLT with natural language output
   on consumer hardware has not been demonstrated in literature.
   
   OUR SOLUTION: Pipeline B runs 100% offline — MediaPipe (local) + hybrid
   Keras model (local) + FLAN-T5-Small (local CPU) + pyttsx3 (local TTS).
   Zero internet dependency.

   GAP 5: END-TO-END ACCESSIBILITY TOOL (NOT JUST RESEARCH DEMO)
   -------------------------------------------------------------
   Problem: Academic papers demonstrate models in isolation — they show
   accuracy numbers but do not package a complete usable application with GUI,
   TTS audio output, and real-world usability features.
   
   Status: No publicly available, complete sign language translation
   APPLICATION (as opposed to research code) exists for large vocabulary ASL.
   
   OUR SOLUTION: Two full GUIs (offline_pipeline_app.py + live_inference_gui.py)
   with TTS audio output, threading for smooth UX, and gesture-based control.

   GAP 6: MULTI-MODAL FUSION (HAND + FACE + BODY) AT EDGE
   -------------------------------------------------------
   Problem: SignBERT+ and other hand-only systems miss non-manual markers
   (facial expressions, mouthing, eyebrow raises) which are grammatically
   meaningful in ASL. Fusing all modalities at real-time speeds is unsolved.
   
   Status: Multi-modal systems exist but are computationally expensive
   (cannot run real-time on CPU).
   
   OUR CONTRIBUTION: We extract 99 pose landmarks (body + partial face) in
   addition to 126 hand landmarks — a lightweight form of multi-modal fusion
   that runs in real-time via MediaPipe Holistic on standard CPU.

SECTION 4: CURRENT LIMITATIONS OF EXISTING APPROACHES
================================================================================

Q: What are the limitations of current state-of-the-art methods?
----------------------------------------------------------------

LIMITATION 1: DATA SCARCITY AND ANNOTATION COST
   - Sign language datasets are 100-1000x smaller than speech/text datasets
   - Gloss annotation requires Deaf linguistic experts — extremely expensive
   - Most datasets cover only 1-2 sign languages (ASL, German, British)
   - Regional dialects within the same sign language are largely ignored
   
LIMITATION 2: LAB-TO-REAL-WORLD GENERALIZATION
   - Most datasets recorded in controlled lab conditions (fixed background,
     uniform lighting, professional signers)
   - Models trained on lab data fail on real-world webcam footage
   - ASL Citizen (NeurIPS 2023) is a first step, but still limited

LIMITATION 3: COMPUTATIONAL COST
   - High-accuracy models (I3D, Video Transformers) require GPUs and large
     memory — cannot deploy on laptops, phones, or embedded devices
   - Inference latency of 200ms-2 seconds per video clip breaks real-time UX
   - Model size: I3D models can be 90MB-300MB+ — difficult to distribute

LIMITATION 4: THE GLOSS BOTTLENECK
   - Most systems require manual gloss annotations during TRAINING
   - Gloss annotations are language-specific and not generalizable
   - Gloss-free systems (like SignLLM/CVPR 2024) exist but require
     massive compute for training (not feasible for student projects)

LIMITATION 5: SINGLE MODALITY
   - Video-based systems (I3D) ignore pose/skeleton information
   - Pose-based systems ignore visual texture and appearance
   - True multimodal fusion remains computationally expensive

LIMITATION 6: EVALUATION METRIC LIMITATIONS
   - Top-1 accuracy on isolated sign datasets doesn't capture real-world
     performance (continuous signing, co-articulation, signing speed variation)
   - BLEU score for translation is a poor proxy for linguistic quality
     (high BLEU doesn't mean the sentence sounds natural)
   - No standardized benchmark for end-to-end SLT with audio output

LIMITATION 7: ABSENCE OF CONTINUOUS SIGN LANGUAGE IN MOST SYSTEMS
   - Most datasets and models handle ISOLATED signs (one sign at a time)
   - Continuous Sign Language Recognition (CSLR) — where signs flow together
     without pauses — is significantly harder and less solved
   - Our system currently handles isolated signs (with temporal windowing),
     not true continuous signing

SECTION 5: EVALUATION METRICS USED IN THIS FIELD
================================================================================

Q: What metrics are used to evaluate sign language systems?
----------------------------------------------------------

FOR SIGN LANGUAGE RECOGNITION (SLR):
-------------------------------------
1. TOP-1 ACCURACY
   Definition: Percentage of test samples where the correct class is
   the model's top prediction.
   Used in: WLASL benchmark (our offline pipeline evaluation).
   Our pipeline: ~50-65% on WLASL-2000 (industry norm for 2000 classes).

2. TOP-5 ACCURACY (or TOP-K)
   Definition: Correct class appears in model's top-5 predictions.
   Used in: Large vocabulary tasks where Top-1 alone is too strict.
   ASL Citizen: ~91% Recall@10 (top-10 retrieval).

3. F1-SCORE (Per Class)
   Definition: Harmonic mean of Precision and Recall for each class.
   Formula: F1 = 2 * (Precision * Recall) / (Precision + Recall)
   Our usage: WE FILTER CLASSES BY F1 >= 0.70 for the live model.
   This is our novel contribution — most papers don't filter by F1 for
   deployment. It ensures our live predictions are reliable.

4. PRECISION
   Definition: Of all times the model predicted class X, how often was it X?
   High precision = fewer false alarms.

5. RECALL
   Definition: Of all actual class X samples, how many did the model catch?
   High recall = model doesn't miss signs.

6. CONFUSION MATRIX
   Definition: Grid showing which classes get confused with each other.
   Useful for identifying visually similar signs that the model struggles with.


FOR SIGN LANGUAGE TRANSLATION (SLT):
--------------------------------------
7. BLEU SCORE (Bilingual Evaluation Understudy)
   Definition: Measures n-gram overlap between predicted sentence and reference.
   Range: 0 (no match) to 100 (perfect match).
   Standard: BLEU-4 (4-gram) used on PHOENIX-2014T benchmark.
   Limitation: High BLEU doesn't guarantee natural-sounding output.

8. ROUGE SCORE
   Definition: Measures recall of n-grams in reference that appear in output.
   Used for: Translation quality evaluation alongside BLEU.

9. WER — WORD ERROR RATE
   Definition: Percentage of words in output that differ from reference.
   Lower is better. Used for CTC-based continuous sign recognition.

10. CER — CHARACTER ERROR RATE  
    Similar to WER but at character level. More granular.


FOR OUR PROJECT (PRACTICAL METRICS):
--------------------------------------
11. REAL-TIME FPS (Frames Per Second)
    Definition: How many frames the system processes per second.
    Our target: 25-30 FPS for smooth live inference.
    Our achievement: Inference every 3 frames with 50-frame buffer
    = effectively real-time on consumer CPU.

12. LATENCY
    Definition: Time delay between signing and displaying translated output.
    Pipeline A (cloud): 2-5 seconds (includes API call)
    Pipeline B (offline): <1 second (fully local)

13. CLASSIFICATION REPORT
    Our hybrid model evaluated using sklearn classification_report giving
    per-class Precision, Recall, F1, and Support. This is how we selected
    the 300-class subset.

SECTION 6: WHAT OUR PROJECT ACHIEVES (CONTRIBUTIONS)
================================================================================

Q: What does your project contribute beyond existing research?
-------------------------------------------------------------

CONTRIBUTION 1: DUAL-PIPELINE ARCHITECTURE (Novel System Design)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
No existing published work combines:
  (a) A high-accuracy cloud-assisted pipeline (I3D + commercial LLM API)
  (b) A fully offline edge pipeline (keypoints + local LLM)
  ...in a single deployable application for ASL recognition.

This dual-architecture directly addresses the accuracy-vs-efficiency tradeoff
that the WLASL and ASL Citizen papers identify as an open problem.

Pipeline A: Optimized for accuracy, accepts pre-recorded/offline video
Pipeline B: Optimized for privacy and latency, works with live webcam

CONTRIBUTION 2: LLM-BASED GLOSS-TO-SENTENCE PIPELINE (Practical NLP Bridge)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
Most SLR systems output raw glosses (e.g., THANK + YOU + HELP).
We implement two gloss-to-sentence pathways:
  (a) Cloud: Gemini 2.5 Flash via zero-shot prompting — highest quality
  (b) Offline: FLAN-T5-Small via HuggingFace — fully local, no internet

This directly bridges the "gloss problem" (a known research gap since 2018)
using modern LLM technology, making our system the first student project to
demonstrate gloss-to-sentence translation at this scale.

CONTRIBUTION 3: F1-SCORE BASED CLASS FILTERING (Practical Engineering)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Our 300-class real-time model is built by:
  Step 1: Train on all available ASL Citizen classes
  Step 2: Evaluate per-class F1-Score using classification_report
  Step 3: Keep ONLY classes with F1 >= 0.70
  Step 4: Retrain/filter the deployment model to these 300 classes

This is a practical, data-driven approach to reducing false positives in live
deployment — addressing the signer-independence gap in a measurable way.
No published paper specifically proposes F1-filtered subset selection for
live sign language deployment.

CONTRIBUTION 4: FULLY OFFLINE END-TO-END TTS PIPELINE
------------------------------------------------------
Our Pipeline B achieves complete offline operation:
  MediaPipe (local) → Hybrid Keras Model (local) → FLAN-T5 (local CPU)
  → pyttsx3 TTS (local) → Audio Output

This makes the system viable for:
  - Rural/remote areas (no internet)
  - Privacy-sensitive environments (healthcare, legal)
  - Embedded / low-connectivity deployments

CONTRIBUTION 5: HEURISTIC SPATIAL GESTURE CONTROL
-------------------------------------------------
We implement programmatic gesture-based UI control using MediaPipe coordinates:
  - Thumbs Up gesture (thumb tip Y < wrist Y by threshold) → Speak
  - Open Palm (all fingertips spread, all extended) → Clear Buffer

This is an accessibility-first design — a deaf user controls the entire
application using only their hands, without needing keyboard or mouse.

CONTRIBUTION 6: THREADED ASYNC ARCHITECTURE FOR REAL-TIME GUI
------------------------------------------------------------
Deep learning inference and LLM API calls are blocking operations.
We use Python threading to offload all inference to daemon threads,
keeping the GUI completely responsive. This software engineering contribution
ensures the system is production-usable, not just a research prototype.

SECTION 7: WHAT OUR PROJECT DOES NOT ACHIEVE (Honest Limitations)
================================================================================

Q: What are the limitations of YOUR project / what remains as future work?
-------------------------------------------------------------------------

CURRENT LIMITATION 1: ISOLATED SIGNS ONLY
   Our system recognizes one sign at a time (isolated sign recognition).
   True continuous signing (where signs blend without pauses) is NOT supported.
   Future Work: Add CTC (Connectionist Temporal Classification) decoder
   or transformer sequence model for continuous sign recognition.

CURRENT LIMITATION 2: ASL-ONLY
   Both pipelines are trained exclusively on American Sign Language (ASL).
   Other sign languages (BSL, ISL, Indian SL, Arabic SL) are not supported.
   Future Work: Extend to multi-sign-language support using transfer learning.

CURRENT LIMITATION 3: NON-MANUAL MARKERS NOT FULLY CAPTURED
   Facial expressions (frowning = question, raised brows = yes/no question)
   are grammatically important in ASL but not fully modeled in our system.
   MediaPipe Holistic captures partial face landmarks but our model doesn't
   specifically weight facial expression features.
   Future Work: Add dedicated face landmark encoding with emotional attention.

CURRENT LIMITATION 4: SIGNING SPEED SENSITIVITY
   Very fast or very slow signers may not align with the 50-frame buffer
   timing assumptions of Pipeline B, reducing accuracy.
   Future Work: Dynamic temporal scaling / speed normalization preprocessing.

CURRENT LIMITATION 5: VOCABULARY GAP (Pipeline B)
   Pipeline B covers only 300 classes vs Pipeline A's 2000 classes.
   A user wanting to sign a word outside the 300-class set gets no output.
   Future Work: Expand to 1000+ classes with hardware acceleration (GPU/NPU).

CURRENT LIMITATION 6: NO SIGN LANGUAGE PRODUCTION (REVERSE DIRECTION)
   Our system translates SIGN → TEXT/AUDIO.
   The reverse (TEXT → SIGN LANGUAGE VIDEO) — called Sign Language Production
   (SLP) — is not implemented.
   Future Work: Integrate avatar-based SLP using diffusion models (e.g., 
   Neural Sign Actors, CVPR 2024).

CURRENT LIMITATION 7: FLAN-T5-SMALL QUALITY
   The offline LLM (FLAN-T5-Small, ~77M parameters) produces grammatically
   reasonable but sometimes awkward sentences compared to Gemini API.
   "THANK YOU HELP ME" → "Thank you for helping." (Gemini: excellent)
                       → "Thank you help me." (FLAN-T5-Small: imperfect)
   Future Work: Upgrade to FLAN-T5-Large or fine-tune on sign-language
   gloss-to-sentence pairs for better offline quality.

SECTION 8: BROAD RESEARCH Q&A
================================================================================

Q: What is the current state of the art in Sign Language Recognition?
---------------------------------------------------------------------
A: As of 2024-2025, the state-of-the-art for isolated word-level ASL
   recognition on WLASL-2000 achieves ~55-60% Top-1 accuracy. For continuous
   SLT on PHOENIX-2014T, top systems achieve BLEU-4 ~29-32. The trend has
   shifted from CNN-based models (I3D, ResNet) to Transformer-based models
   with self-supervised pre-training (e.g., SignBERT+, UniSign/ICLR 2025).
   LLMs are now being integrated for gloss-free translation (SignLLM, CVPR 2024).

Q: Why is Sign Language Translation harder than Speech Recognition?
-------------------------------------------------------------------
A: Several reasons make SLT fundamentally harder:
   1. VISUAL MODALITY: Video is much higher-dimensional than audio waveforms.
      Extracting meaning from pixels is computationally expensive.
   2. GRAMMAR MISMATCH: ASL grammar ≠ English grammar. Word order differs,
      there are no articles (a, the), tense is expressed differently.
   3. CO-ARTICULATION: In continuous signing, signs blend together — similar
      to how spoken words run together in natural speech, but in 3D space.
   4. NON-MANUAL MARKERS: Facial expressions, head tilt, and body posture
      carry grammatical meaning — hard to capture with standard cameras.
   5. DATA SCARCITY: Speech recognition has billions of audio-text pairs.
      Sign language has thousands of video-gloss pairs at most.
   6. SIGNER VARIABILITY: Every person's hands are different size, shape,
      and signing style — far more variation than speaker voice differences.

Q: What role do Large Language Models (LLMs) play in this field?
---------------------------------------------------------------
A: LLMs are increasingly central to Sign Language Translation:
   1. GLOSS-TO-SENTENCE (our approach): Convert raw gloss sequences to
      fluent English. LLMs excel at this due to strong language priors.
   2. GLOSS-FREE TRANSLATION (SignLLM, CVPR 2024): Map visual features
      directly to LLM token space, bypassing gloss annotations entirely.
   3. DATA AUGMENTATION: LLMs generate synthetic gloss-sentence pairs
      to expand limited training data.
   4. EVALUATION: LLMs evaluate translation quality better than BLEU score.
   
   Our project uses LLMs for approach #1 — the most practical and deployable
   use case that doesn't require retraining a massive model.

Q: What is the "Gloss Problem" in Sign Language Translation?
------------------------------------------------------------
A: Glosses are text representations of signs (one word per sign, in signing
   order). The "Gloss Problem" is the disconnect between gloss sequences and
   natural spoken language:
   
   GLOSS SEQUENCE:  [DOCTOR + TOMORROW + APPOINTMENT + ME + NEED]
   ENGLISH SENTENCE: "I have a doctor's appointment tomorrow."
   
   Challenges:
   - Word order is reversed or scrambled
   - Grammatical words (articles, prepositions) are often omitted in signs
   - Tense must be inferred from temporal indicators not direct glosses
   - Sign language has no direct written form — glosses are a compromise
   
   Most recognition systems solve SLR (producing the gloss sequence) but NOT
   SLT (producing the English sentence). Our project solves BOTH.

Q: What evaluation benchmarks are standard in this field?
---------------------------------------------------------
A: 
   FOR ISOLATED SLR:
   - WLASL (100 / 300 / 1000 / 2000 class subsets) — ASL
   - MSASL (1000 classes) — ASL
   - ASL Citizen (2731 classes, signer-independent) — ASL
   - NMFs-CSL (500 classes) — Chinese Sign Language
   - SLR500 — Chinese Sign Language

   FOR CONTINUOUS SLR & TRANSLATION:
   - PHOENIX-2014T — German Sign Language (continuous, weather forecast domain)
   - CSL-Daily — Chinese Sign Language (daily conversation)
   - SP-10 — 10 Sign Languages (multilingual benchmark)
   - DGS Corpus — German Sign Language (large scale)
   - BOBSL — British Sign Language (BBC subtitled)

Q: How does Transfer Learning help in this field?
------------------------------------------------
A: Transfer learning is critical because sign language datasets are tiny
   compared to general video datasets.

   The typical pipeline:
   1. Pre-train on large general datasets (Kinetics for I3D, ImageNet for CNN)
   2. Fine-tune on sign language datasets (WLASL, ASL Citizen)
   
   Our offline pipeline specifically uses I3D weights pre-trained on BSL-1K
   (British Sign Language) and then fine-tuned on WLASL-2000. This multi-step
   transfer provides two domain-specific inductive biases before final training.
   
   SignBERT+ extends this: self-supervised pre-training on ANY hand video
   (unlabeled) → fine-tuning on labeled sign data. This is especially powerful
   when labeled sign data is scarce.

Q: What hardware is typically required for sign language systems?
----------------------------------------------------------------
A: Academic systems often require:
   - High-end GPUs (V100, A100) for training
   - GPU workstations (RTX 3080+) for inference in published papers
   
   Our system runs on:
   Pipeline A: Standard laptop CPU (I3D runs on CPU, slower but functional)
               OR cloud GPU via API (faster)
   Pipeline B: Standard laptop CPU + webcam (MediaPipe + Keras run on CPU)
               FLAN-T5-Small runs on CPU (quantized, ~1GB RAM)
   
   This hardware accessibility is a key practical contribution of our project.

Q: What is the role of MediaPipe in modern sign language research?
-----------------------------------------------------------------
A: MediaPipe (Google's real-time ML framework) provides:
   - 21 3D hand landmarks per hand (42 total both hands)
   - 33 body pose landmarks
   - Face mesh (468 landmarks)
   - Runs at 30+ FPS on CPU using mobile-optimized models
   
   Its importance in SLR research:
   - Enables real-time pose extraction WITHOUT specialized hardware
   - Makes keypoint-based approaches viable on consumer devices
   - Provides normalized, interpretable features vs. opaque pixel features
   - Privacy-preserving: only coordinates are processed, not raw faces/hands
   
   SignBERT+ (TPAMI 2023) validates the research value of this representation.
   Our Pipeline B is built entirely around MediaPipe Holistic output.

Q: What is the significance of the BSL-1K dataset in your project?
-----------------------------------------------------------------
A: BSL-1K (Albanie et al., ECCV 2020) is a large British Sign Language dataset
   used to pre-train the I3D weights that power our offline pipeline.
   
   Our offline model uses a 3-stage transfer learning chain:
   Stage 1: Pre-trained on Kinetics (general action recognition, ~400 classes)
   Stage 2: Fine-tuned on BSL-1K (British Sign Language, 1000 classes)
   Stage 3: Fine-tuned on WLASL-2000 (American Sign Language, 2000 classes)
   
   This progressive domain adaptation — from general actions → BSL → ASL —
   gives our model much richer sign-specific features than starting from
   Kinetics directly. The BSL-1K weights are accessed via our 'bsl1k/' module.
